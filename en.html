THE PROBLEM

Two groups who need each other, and no market between them

In Baku you can have a restaurant meal delivered in thirty minutes. You cannot buy the dolma someone's mother made this morning — the food most people in this city would actually rather eat.

On the other side of that gap are thousands of people, most of them women, who cook to a professional standard every single day and have never earned a manat from it. Not because the skill is missing. Because no one built the market.

Restaurant delivery cannot serve this

Wolt, Bolt Food and Yango are built on restaurant partners — commercial kitchens, commercial licences, commercial menus. A home kitchen does not fit that model, and adapting to it would mean rebuilding the business they already have.

The work is invisible, so it is unpaid

A woman who has cooked for her family for twenty-eight years has a skill worth money and no way to sell it. Duemo does not ask her to learn a new trade or pass an interview. She cooks what she already cooks, for people who want it.

The category does not exist here yet

We reviewed thirteen platforms across the US, India, Russia and Europe. Home-cook marketplaces work elsewhere. In Azerbaijan, the Caucasus and Central Asia, nobody is running one.

THE MODEL

Where the money comes from, and why the unit economics hold

Three revenue lines, one structural cost advantage. All of it is decided and visible in the product, not planned for later.

20 → 15%

Commission that falls as a cook grows

20% for a new cook, 17% at Silver, 15% at Gold — gated on quality score and monthly orders, not on negotiation. The cook sees the full breakdown before accepting. We are deliberately avoiding the moment where a platform's best suppliers decide it is working against them.

−76%

Batched delivery, not per-order delivery

One courier, one building, several orders in the same window. Our internal routing model puts the cost of a single delivery at 2.50 ₼ and a batched one at 0.60 ₼ — roughly 76% lower. That gap is the business: it is what lets home-cooked food undercut restaurant delivery instead of costing more than it.

B2B

B2B is the margin engine

Office lunch programmes and catering land several orders at one address in one window, which is the batching model at its best case. B2B revenue is what makes the consumer commission sustainable rather than the other way round.

Every figure on this page comes from Duemo's own routing model and commission structure. We will walk any of it line by line.

WHY NOW

An uncontested category in a market that already behaves the right way

Nobody is competing for it

No home-cook marketplace operates in Azerbaijan, Georgia or Armenia. The incumbents are restaurant platforms and structurally stay that way. We are not fighting for share in a category — we are opening one.

The behaviour is already there

Home cooking is not a trend to be created in Azerbaijan; it is the default. People already prefer it and already sell it informally between neighbours. What has been missing is trust at a distance, payment, and delivery — which is exactly what a marketplace provides.

The model travels

The same conditions hold across the Caucasus and Central Asia: strong home-cooking culture, high mobile penetration, no incumbent. Baku is the first market, not the only one.
